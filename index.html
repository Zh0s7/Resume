ABOUT

A bit about me.

Mechatronic Systems Engineering
Minor in Computing Science
Simon Fraser University · Class of 2028

I like understanding how things work well enough to take them apart, find what needs to change, and put them back together. Sometimes that means inspecting worn engine components. Other times it means working through firmware until a motor behaves the way it should.

That interest shows up in a pretty wide range of projects. I independently rebuilt a Honda D15B2 engine, and I’m working on a hybrid Mazda 13B rotary engine and an electric go-kart. Those builds involve more than choosing parts: they mean checking fit and tolerances, researching how components interact, troubleshooting, and figuring out what I can actually test.

With Otis Dynamics, I’ve been able to bring that hands-on approach into a collaborative robotics project. My main ownership has been the mobile-base firmware and motor-controller integration. I’m now researching how embedded magnets and magnetic-field sensors could help a soft gripper detect changes in a grasp. I also contribute to vehicle dynamics work with SFU Team Phantom.

The computing side matters to me just as much. My projects include warehouse robotics simulation, computer vision, a RISC-V emulator, and self-hosted computing infrastructure. Studying mechatronics with a minor in Computing Science lets me keep exploring both the physical system and the software behind it.

I enjoy having a problem that takes more than one kind of engineering to solve. This portfolio is a record of what I’ve built, what I’ve learned, and what I’m still working through.

Platform and engineering objective

The team collaboratively designed, fabricated, and assembled a wheeled research robot with a BLDC drivetrain, an extendable torso, an articulated sensor head, and an arm with human-arm-like articulation. The platform incorporates a hand and gripper, LiDAR, depth-camera perception, ROS 2, and RViz.

The work explores mobility and manipulation capabilities before selecting a specific commercial application. Joint count, payload capacity, and full hardware specifications remain undocumented.

My individual ownership

My primary contribution to the existing platform was mobile-base firmware and motor-controller integration. I worked on low-level firmware, drivetrain bring-up, BLDC control research, and debugging the motor interfaces before higher-level ROS 2 and RViz integration.

I also contributed to team design discussions, fabrication, assembly, sensor integration, navigation development, and system testing. The existing arm and hand were developed collaboratively, with primary design ownership held by other team members.

Current research

I am researching soft grippers containing embedded magnets and magnetic-field sensors. Deformation or displacement of the magnets may reveal changes at the contact interface, including object movement, slipping, and soft-element deformation.

Grasp-state estimation and adaptive manipulation are research objectives. The sensing system is still under investigation, with development, validation, and integration outstanding.

Tools and methods

Embedded firmware, BLDC motor controllers, ROS 2, RViz, LiDAR, depth cameras, electromechanical integration, soft robotics, magnetic sensing, fabrication, and collaborative prototyping.

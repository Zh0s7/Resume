AWD Electric Go-Kart

Independent wheel drives and planned torque vectoring

Vehicle architecture

The design combines four 6.5-inch hoverboard BLDC hub motors, four ZS-X11H controllers, a Teensy 4.0, and a 36 V lithium-ion power system. Integrated Hall-effect feedback supports the wheel drives.

The chassis concept uses a repurposed shopping-cart basket with a custom supporting frame. SolidWorks work covers mounting, a mechanical front steering system, rack-and-pinion architecture, steering-column components, and Ackermann geometry research.

My completed work

I independently developed and tested the Teensy firmware and controller interfaces, brought up the electronics, characterized motor behavior, and debugged low-level electrical integration. Motor-speed, direction, and electronic braking controls are functioning.

Vehicle-level integration and validation remain outstanding. Chassis fabrication and mechanical assembly are the next major milestones.

Active sensing and integration

Current design work includes magnetic absolute steering-angle sensing, ACS758 current sensing, individual motor-current monitoring, wheel-speed feedback, and electronic accelerator sensing. These are integration workstreams with completion still pending.

Planned control system

After mechanical assembly, I plan to develop independent torque distribution across all four wheels using steering position, accelerator demand, wheel behavior, and motor-current feedback.

Torque-vectoring algorithms and their experimental validation remain future work. A custom VCU PCB is also planned, with design and manufacture outstanding. The current architecture uses direct motor-controller interfaces.

Tools and methods

Teensy 4.0, embedded C/C++, PWM, BLDC controllers, Hall-effect sensing, SolidWorks, magnetic encoders, current sensing, vehicle dynamics, steering geometry, and electromechanical integration.

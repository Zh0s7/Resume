Custom Hybrid Mazda 13B

Cross-generation rotary powertrain development

A hybrid rotary-engine build for a 2006 Mazda RX-8, investigating component compatibility, sealing, intake staging, and thermal management.

Architecture and motivation

I am investigating alternatives to the factory 13B-MSP Renesis architecture, with emphasis on exhaust flow, thermal loading, sealing, and high-RPM operation.

The proposed hybrid combines Renesis side irons and high-compression RX-8 rotors with peripheral-exhaust housings from the GSL-SE or FD3S 13B-REW family. Final donor-housing selection remains open.

Completed teardown and ongoing work

I independently completed the 13B-MSP teardown and inspected the internal components and sealing systems. Rebuild, manufacturing, and testing activities are in progress. Individual manufacturing milestones and experimental results still require documentation.

My work includes dimensional compatibility research, component inspection, failure analysis, tolerance considerations, machining development, and fluid and thermal system architecture.

Sealing and rotor compatibility

Cross-generation coolant-seal geometries require careful metrology, tolerance analysis, and custom interface development. Rotor and apex-seal compatibility introduces additional clearance, material, wear, and thermal considerations.

The custom sealing architecture remains subject to manufacturing inspection and testing. Reliability and wear improvements are engineering objectives awaiting experimental evidence.

Intake and thermal research

The current intake concept combines staged side-intake ports with additional peripheral-intake functionality and electronic port switching. It aims to balance low-speed drivability with high-RPM airflow.

A dual parallel electric coolant-pump concept investigates flow control independent of engine RPM and post-shutdown heat management. Auxiliary charge-cooling and combustion-stability concepts are also being researched. Their performance has yet to be established.

Current scope and limitations

Airflow, volumetric efficiency, cooling, and longevity improvements remain hypotheses for validation. Earlier iris-style intake, three-rotor, and forced-induction investigations are historical concepts outside the current defined hybrid architecture.
